# Acceptable Use Policy

_Last updated: November 3, 2026_

os-lab exists for defensive security work, research and teaching in isolated
labs. This policy describes the use the project supports. It does not add
conditions to the [Apache License 2.0](../../LICENSE); it states what the
maintainers will and will not help with, and the law already restricts much of
the prohibited use below.

## Supported use

- Security assessment of applications, devices and systems you own, or that
  you are authorized in writing to test, within the agreed scope and time.
- Authorized bug-bounty work within a program's published scope and rules.
- Malware analysis and reverse engineering inside isolated guests.
- Teaching, training and capture-the-flag exercises on targets built for them.
- Defensive research, detection engineering and compatibility testing.

## Not supported

- Intercepting, instrumenting, patching or testing apps, devices, accounts or
  networks without authorization, or outside the authorized scope.
- Collecting other people's credentials, messages, location or other personal
  data, or monitoring someone without their knowledge or a lawful basis.
- Building, distributing or operating malware, stalkerware or tools meant to
  cause harm, and using lab guests to attack third parties.
- Bypassing licensing, DRM, payment or anti-cheat controls of software you do
  not have rights to, and redistributing patched applications.
- Running Windows or macOS without the licences their terms require (see the
  [Terms of Use](TERMS.md), section 4).
- Exposing lab services or the web console to the internet, or letting
  untrusted users reach them.

Issues, pull requests and discussions that ask for help with unsupported use
will be closed.

## Responsible disclosure

If your work reveals a vulnerability in someone else's app or system, do not
exploit it beyond what is needed to confirm it and do not publish details
before the owner has had a reasonable chance to fix it. Report it privately
through the owner's security contact, `security.txt` or bug-bounty program.

## Vulnerabilities in os-lab

Report them privately as described in [SECURITY.md](../../SECURITY.md), not in
public issues.
